The five-question lens

Use the same causal framework for any country. Statistics are evidence; these questions explain the mechanism.

01

What is sold?

Identify tradable goods, services, resources and intellectual property—not merely the largest domestic sectors.

02

Why here?

Look for geography, skills, supplier clusters, institutions, scale, research or accumulated production knowledge.

03

Where is the bottleneck?

The highest-value layer may be design, a machine, a material, certification, logistics, finance or the customer relationship.

04

Who captures the rent?

“Rent” means income above the minimum needed to keep a resource or capability in use. Ownership determines who receives it.

05

What breaks first?

Every model has a failure mode: debt, demographics, energy, trade concentration, weak institutions or technological substitution.

Memory method

For each country, recall three items:

  1. Object: one concrete product or service.
  2. Moat: why copying it takes years.
  3. Failure: the model’s first weak point.

Example: Netherlands → EUV machine → supplier/optics ecosystem → export controls and trade fragmentation.

A four-week learning path

About 25 minutes per day. The goal is to form causal models, not memorize rankings.

Week 1

Learn the grammar

Read CORE Econ selections and watch MRU trade videos.

  • Productivity
  • Comparative advantage
  • Scale and clusters
  • Institutions and rents
Week 2

Read trade maps

Choose five countries in OEC and the Harvard Atlas.

  • Top exports
  • Top partners
  • Diversification
  • Adjacent capabilities
Week 3

Diagnose constraints

Read one OECD or IMF executive summary per day.

  • Demography
  • Debt and inflation
  • Housing and labour
  • Energy and trade exposure
Week 4

Follow one product

Trace an AI server, car, drug or tonne of steel across countries.

  • Who designs?
  • Who manufactures?
  • Who finances?
  • Who owns the customer?
